Architecture in practice

My job is not to produce diagrams. It is to help teams make better technical decisions.

I operate where architecture becomes executable: clarifying boundaries, evaluating trade-offs, aligning teams, designing integration paths, making quality attributes explicit and ensuring that a solution can actually be delivered and operated.

01

Architecture direction

I help translate business goals and constraints into technical direction, decomposing ambiguous problems into decisions teams can implement incrementally.

02

Distributed systems

I work with asynchronous flows, messaging, idempotency, consistency, caching, resilience and failure modes — especially where several systems and teams must collaborate.

03

APIs & integrations

I design boundaries, contracts and integration strategies with attention to coupling, ownership, latency, partial failures, versioning and long-term evolution.

04

Cloud & production readiness

I treat deployment, scalability, observability, security, infrastructure constraints and operational support as architecture concerns from the beginning.

05

Domain modeling

I use Domain-Driven Design pragmatically to expose business concepts, clarify responsibilities and create boundaries that reduce accidental complexity.

06

Governance & enablement

I prefer guardrails over gates: ADRs, standards, quality automation, code review, templates and technical guidance that help teams move with more autonomy.

How I think about architecture

Architecture is a continuous decision system.

The value is not in the diagram itself. It is in making decisions explicit, testing assumptions early, understanding constraints and creating feedback loops that keep the system evolvable.

01

Start from the problem, not the pattern

Patterns are vocabulary, not objectives. I begin with business context, system forces and quality attributes before selecting technology or structure.

02

Constraints are part of the design

Legacy systems, deadlines, organizational boundaries, cloud capabilities, compliance and team maturity are not side notes — they shape the architecture.

03

Design for failure and operation

Timeouts, retries, observability, partial results, idempotency, recovery and supportability deserve the same attention as the happy path.

04

Governance should create feedback, not friction

Good governance makes the right path easier through automation, conventions, evidence and shared decision records instead of unnecessary ceremony.

05

Stay close enough to the code

I use implementation, prototypes, libraries and tooling to validate architectural ideas and keep decisions grounded in engineering reality.

About

From writing code to shaping the systems around it.

I am Rodrigo de Oliveira, a Software Architect with more than 20 years in software development. My career has given me the perspective of someone who still understands the cost of a decision at code level, while now working with the wider consequences of that decision across systems, teams and operations.

Today, my work is less about choosing a fashionable technology and more about creating clarity: understanding what matters, identifying the real constraints, exposing trade-offs and helping engineering teams move toward solutions that are technically sound and operationally sustainable.

I remain hands-on because architecture benefits from proximity to implementation. Open-source libraries, analyzers, templates and architecture tools are part of how I explore ideas, test assumptions and share what I learn.
